The Challenge

Severstal — one of Russia's largest steel producers — had a youth problem. The factory floor wasn't competing with tech startups for Gen Z talent. The brand felt distant, industrial, uninviting. How do you make a steel mill exciting to someone who grew up on gaming?

The task: attract young candidates and make Severstal feel like a place they'd actually want to work. Not through a careers page. Through something they'd choose to spend time with.

The Idea

We turned the factory into a game world. Real Severstal objects became in-game artifacts — cranes, blast furnaces, coil rolls, casting equipment. Players hunted for them across popular video games: finding a real factory object in a game environment, photographing it, uploading it to the platform.

The concept: Steel Hunters. Different games, one goal. The hunt ran in rounds across five weeks, with a live leaderboard, community voting, and prizes. A Twitch streamer with 126K followers joined the hunt live — the platform briefly appeared on his stream to 77,000 viewers.

How It Worked

Participants found Severstal production objects hidden inside popular video game environments — Escape from Tarkov, CS2, open-world titles. Each round had a new target object and a fresh leaderboard.

Screenshots went to the platform. Community voted on the best finds. Top hunters earned prizes. The campaign ran five rounds over five weeks, each building on the last — new targets, new tension, new winners.

Why It Worked

Because it didn't feel like recruitment. It felt like a game. The audience participated because they wanted to — not because they were looking for a job. And yet by the end, they knew Severstal's production equipment by heart, had visited the platform 163,900 times, and 1,248 of them had submitted their CVs.

2.5% of Severstal's 50,000-person workforce engaged with the campaign. A Twitch streamer played it live to 77,000 people. Content spread through gaming communities without a media buy.
