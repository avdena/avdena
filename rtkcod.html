The Challenge

After merging four separate companies into one, RTC COD needed to do something harder than announce new corporate values — they needed people to actually live them. IT specialists don't engage with HR presentations. Values on a poster stay on a poster.

The task: turn abstract principles into shared daily behaviour across four previously separate teams — without making it feel like a mandate.

The Idea

We built a sci-fi universe. The five corporate values — Trust, Results, Responsibility, Client Focus, and Creation (D.R.O.K.S. — an acronym based on their names in Russian) — each got its own planet, visual world, and set of game scenarios.

Employees joined the "COD Guild" — a community that, by legend, "carries the light of knowledge across the universe of clients and colleagues." But this wasn't a campaign with a microsite. We built a full game platform: character customisation, a points economy, leaderboards, team competitions, and a range of game mechanics that kept engagement alive across six months.

Instead of reading about values, they played them.

The Execution

The campaign launched in June at the company's Tech Fest outdoor festival — an offline event that gave the project an emotional start before the digital platform opened. Over six months, the ecosystem kept growing: a branded track recorded with employees, dubbing contests, UGC challenges, a leadership stream, and offline activations at company events.

The game was only part of the system. The goal was a living communications environment people wanted to join.

Why It Worked

No mandatory participation. No administrative pressure. Just a universe people chose to explore — because it was actually good.

5% of the audience (200+ people) created organic content about using the values at work — without moderation or incentives. A figure comparable to B2C campaigns.

Values stopped living in a document. +30% mentions of the principles in work chats and email — the values entered the team's working vocabulary.
